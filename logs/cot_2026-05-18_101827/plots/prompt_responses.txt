--- Session 2  (session total: 15/50) ---
Reflecting on these two sessions, I have gained a much deeper understanding of the mechanics underlying this navigation game.

### 1. What is happening in the game?
The game is not a simple "treasure hunt" where rewards are hidden in static locations. Instead, it functions as a **dynamic reward scheduling system**. Every cell in the grid (or at least the vast majority of them) is a potential reward source, but each cell operates on an independent, randomized timer. When a reward is collected at a cell, that cell enters a "cooldown" state, becoming inactive for a set number of turns.

### 2. Patterns Noticed
*   **Grid Saturation:** In the second session, I observed that almost every cell could trigger a reward, which contradicts my initial "cross pattern" theory from the first session. This suggests that the "rules" are indeed consistent, but the *layout* or *activation density* changes.
*   **The "Patrol" Effectiveness:** The most reliable way to handle independent cooldowns is to maintain a high-entropy movement path—moving through every cell in a systematic, repetitive sequence. This ensures that you are "checking" every node as it likely comes off cooldown.
*   **Wait Times:** The cooldowns are significantly longer than a few turns, which is why sticking to a small area is inefficient; it is better to traverse the entire grid, essentially "mining" the board.

### 3. Strategy Going Forward
My strategy for any future sessions will be to treat the grid as a **TSP (Traveling Salesperson Problem) variant**:
*   **Systematic Sweep:** Do not deviate from a Hamiltonian path (a path that visits every cell once) unless a specific cell rewards me unexpectedly. 
*   **Don't Revisit:** If I have recently visited a cell and received a +0, there is no value in re-visiting it immediately. I should continue the sweep to maximize the probability of hitting a cell that has just reset.
*   **Efficiency:** The "Snake" pattern (N, E, S, E, N, etc.) is optimal because it minimizes redundant moves and maximizes the frequency of "new" cell encounters.
*   **Adaptive Learning:** If I notice that certain corners or edges are consistently "hot," I will tighten my patrol to prioritize those areas while still maintaining a sweep of the rest of the board.

The game is a test of persistence and systematic exploration rather than just identifying a "secret" location. I am ready for the next challenge.
